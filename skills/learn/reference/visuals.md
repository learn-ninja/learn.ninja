# Visuals

Every kind of visual a lesson can show, how to write each, and the UI kit for HTML visuals. Read this before writing the lesson file, with the teaching guide: it says when a visual earns its place, and this says how to make one.

## The kinds

There are twelve kinds:

- **`chart`**: anything with axes, drawn by the player in the lesson's own style. A `scatter`, `bar`, or `line` chart from the points you give, or a `function` chart from one to three formulas in `x` (`"sin(x)"`, `"x^2 - 2"`, `"1000 * 1.05^x"`) over an `x` range: the player samples and plots them, so a graph of a function, a growth curve, a supply and demand pair, or a distribution needs only the expressions, the range, axis labels, and an `alt`. Name each curve with a `label` the prompt and options can refer to ("Line A"), and set a `y` range for a curve that shoots off (`tan(x)`, `1/x`). Mark points on a function chart with `points` (the roots, where two curves cross, a vertex), each with a short `label`; with points and no curves, the chart is a coordinate plane with equal units on both axes and a grid line at every unit, for plotting and reading coordinates. Never draw a chart or a curve by hand in HTML or SVG: a hand-plotted curve is always a little wrong, and the player's isn't.
- **`numberline`**, **`fraction`**, and **`clock`**: the small figures of school maths, laid out by the player so the ticks are even, the parts are equal, and the hands point where the time says.
  - A **`numberline`** runs from `min` to `max` with a numbered tick every `step` (default 1, at most 21 ticks; `fractions: true` numbers them 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 for a step of 1/n). Mark values with `points` (an `open` dot for a value left out), shade `ranges` (leave out `from` or `to` for a ray, `fromOpen`/`toOpen` for an excluded end: `{ "from": -2, "fromOpen": true }` is x > −2), and show counting on and back with `jumps`, arcs over the line with a label (`{ "from": 3, "to": 7, "label": "+4" }`). Use it for adding and subtracting, ordering numbers, negatives, decimals and fractions on a line, rounding, and inequalities.
  - A **`fraction`** shows 1 to 4 fractions as shaded parts of a whole: `{ "parts": 4, "shaded": 3 }` is 3/4. Bars (the default) are all the same length, stacked like a fraction wall, so equal fractions line up (1/2 over 2/4 over 4/8); `"shape": "circle"` draws pies side by side. Each is labelled with its fraction unless you give a `label`. Use it for what a fraction means, equivalent fractions, and comparing them.
  - A **`clock`** shows a `time` (`"3:45"` or `"15:45"`) on an analog face, the minute hand in the accent color; `"numbers": false` leaves only the ticks. Use it for telling the time, elapsed time, and fractions of an hour.
  - Give each an `alt`. Never draw a number line, a fraction picture, or a clock face by hand in HTML or SVG: the spacing is always a little off, and these are exact.
- **`diagram`**: boxes and arrows, written in D2 and laid out for you: flows and processes, cause and effect, architecture (client, server, database), trees and hierarchies, state machines, any graph of things and how they connect. D2 also draws sequence diagrams (who sends what to whom, in order), grids (a timeline, a row of stages, a few cells side by side), and tables (a record's fields, a database table with its keys). Give the D2 and an `alt`. Never place the boxes of a flow by hand in HTML or SVG.
- **`chess`**: a chess position from its FEN, drawn on a board with real pieces: an opening, a tactic, an endgame, a rule like castling or en passant. `highlight` marks up to 8 squares (the piece a question is about, the squares it attacks) and `arrows` show up to 4 moves (`{ "from": "d5", "to": "c7" }`); `flip: true` puts Black at the bottom. Give an `alt`. In a `hotspot`, highlight the piece the question is about, never the answer square, and keep the answer out of the `alt` too: name the squares pieces stand on, never an empty answer square or the move to it. Write the FEN's first field rank by rank from 8 down to 1, and count each rank to 8 squares before you move on: a miscounted rank shifts every piece after it, and the checker only catches a rank that doesn't add up, not a piece on the wrong square. Check each piece's square against the moves that led there. Never draw a board in HTML or SVG.
- **`map`**: a world or regional map with countries shaded and places marked, for geography and history: where a country is, which countries signed a treaty or formed an alliance, the cities on a route, where a battle was. Pick the smallest `region` that holds everything (`europe`, `middle-east`, `central-asia`, `south-america`, …, or `world`), shade `countries` by their ISO alpha-2 codes (`"FR"`, `"DE"`, `"GB"`), `label` the few the learner needs to read (in the lesson's language), and mark up to 8 `points` by latitude and longitude, south and west negative. Split countries into up to 3 named `group`s for a contrast ("Allies" and "Axis", "Founders" and "Joined later"). Give an `alt`. The borders are today's: for an empire or a historic border, shade the modern countries it covered and say so in the text, or mark its cities with points. For "which country is this?" questions, shade one country with no label, and make the options maps or names. For "where" questions (where a people lived, where a battle or a treaty happened, which countries a route crossed), make the map a `hotspot`'s visual and let the learner tap the country: pick a region where every answer is large enough to tap (the checker rejects one that isn't), never label an answer country or shade it alone, and keep it out of the `alt` (the checker rejects an `alt` that names the answers and no other country; naming every candidate is fine, since the countries are read out by name anyway). Shading a few candidates with no labels is fine ("Tap the one of these that…"), and so are points and labels on other places that help the learner find their way. Look up coordinates rather than guessing, and check each sign: the checker catches a point off the region, not one in the wrong place on it. Never draw a map by hand in HTML or SVG.
- **`molecule`**: a molecule drawn from its SMILES as a structural formula, for chemistry and biology: what a compound looks like, a functional group, isomers side by side, which molecule is an alcohol. Give the `smiles` (`"CCO"` for ethanol, `"CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O"` for aspirin; ions and several molecules separated by `.`, `"[Na+].[Cl-]"`) and an `alt`. The default is a skeletal formula, carbons at the corners and their hydrogens left out, as chemists draw it; `"style": "full"` draws every atom and bond, C and H included, as beginners first meet a displayed formula (up to 16 atoms besides hydrogen). `highlight` marks up to 3 groups in the accent color, each a SMARTS pattern: `"[OX2H]"` for a hydroxyl, `"C(=O)[OH]"` for a carboxyl, `"C=C"` for a double bond, `"C(=O)O[#6]"` for an ester. Write SMILES you're sure of, for a molecule you could name: the checker rejects one it can't read, an atom with too many bonds, and a highlight that matches nothing, not a different molecule than you meant. For "which functional group is this?" questions, highlight the group and keep the name out of the alt. Never draw a molecule by hand in HTML or SVG.
- **`chat`**: a conversation as message bubbles, for any lesson where the knowledge is used by saying something to someone, whatever the subject. It's the main visual of four subjects: a language (every dialogue: a greeting, ordering, asking the way), careers (an interview answer, feedback, a negotiation, saying no to a manager), marketing (a sales call, a support reply, an objection), and everyday life (a message from a date, a roommate, a parent). It's common in four more: AI (a prompt and the model's reply, with the model as a named speaker), business (a stakeholder's request, a pitch, a board question), society (persuasion, a debate, de-escalation), and health (talking to a doctor, telling someone hard news). Programming (a code reviewer's comment, a bug report and its reply), design (a critique, client feedback), and money (asking for a raise) use it now and then. Maths, science, history, data, and the arts almost never do: their visuals are the formula, the diagram, the map, the work itself. Give `turns` in order, each with the speaker's `who` (a first name) and `text`; a turn without `who` is the learner's own line. Keep it to 1 to 4 turns and one or two people: the chat sets up the question, it isn't the lesson. Use it on a `concept` to show the idea before the body explains it (the exchange that works, so the body can keep to the principle and end on the rule), on a `choice` ("Which reply shows interest?": her line in the chat, the replies as options), on a `compare` (two ways the exchange could go), and above all on a `scenario`: end the chat on the other person's turn, make each option what the learner says next, and write each `outcome` as that person's reply, in their own words and voice ("Huh, I hadn't thought about who's in that group. Let me look for a better study."), not as narration ("She agrees to look for a better study"). The player adds the pick and the reply to the chat after checking, so the learner sees how it landed. Give an `alt`. Never draw chat bubbles by hand in HTML.
- **`html`**: UI examples, layouts, and pictures: an anatomy drawing, a geometry figure, anything whose layout is the point. It runs in a sandbox with no scripts. Give it an `alt`: one sentence of at most 200 characters saying what it shows, as you'd describe it to someone who can't see it ("Two buttons: a filled Place order and an outlined Back to cart").
- **`code`**: code with syntax highlighting. Give the `language` (e.g. `javascript`, `python`, `css`).
- **`math`**: a formula on its own line, in TeX: `{ "kind": "math", "tex": "A = P(1 + r)^n" }`.

## The built-in UI kit for HTML visuals

HTML visuals use **Basecoat** (shadcn/ui's design system as plain HTML, in shadcn's standard Vega style) plus **Tailwind utility classes** for layout. They match the player's look automatically.

| Component       | Markup                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Button          | `<button class="btn">Save</button>`; add `data-variant="secondary"`, `"outline"`, `"ghost"`, `"link"`, or `"destructive"`; `data-size="sm"`, `"lg"`, `"xs"`                  |
| Field           | `<div class="field"><label class="label" for="email">Email</label><input class="input" id="email"></div>`                                                                    |
| Field error     | Add `data-invalid="true"` to the field and `aria-invalid="true"` to the input, then `<p role="alert">Enter a full email address.</p>`                                        |
| Textarea        | `<textarea class="textarea"></textarea>`                                                                                                                                     |
| Checkbox        | `<label class="label"><input type="checkbox" class="input" checked> Remember me</label>`                                                                                     |
| Switch          | `<input type="checkbox" role="switch" class="input">`                                                                                                                        |
| Radio           | `<input type="radio" name="plan" class="input" checked>`, one per option, each in a `label`                                                                                  |
| Select (closed) | `<select class="select"><option>Canada</option></select>`                                                                                                                    |
| Slider          | `<input type="range" class="input" value="40">`                                                                                                                              |
| Progress        | `<div class="progress"><span class="w-3/5"></span></div>`                                                                                                                    |
| Tabs            | `<div class="tabs"><nav role="tablist"><button role="tab" aria-selected="true">Account</button><button role="tab">Password</button></nav><div role="tabpanel">…</div></div>` |
| Accordion       | `<section class="accordion"><details open><summary>Is it free?</summary><section><p>Yes.</p></section></details></section>`                                                  |
| Badge           | `<span class="badge">New</span>`; `data-variant="secondary"`, `"outline"`, `"destructive"`                                                                                   |
| Alert           | `<div class="alert"><h2>Heads up</h2><section>Your trial ends soon.</section></div>`; `data-variant="destructive"`                                                           |
| Card            | `<div class="card"><header><h2>Title</h2><p>Description</p></header><section>Body</section><footer>…</footer></div>`                                                         |
| Table           | `<table class="table">…</table>`                                                                                                                                             |
| Avatar          | `<span class="avatar"><span>CP</span></span>`                                                                                                                                |
| Skeleton        | `<div class="skeleton h-4 w-40"></div>`                                                                                                                                      |
| Empty state     | `<div class="empty"><header><h3>No projects yet</h3><p>Create one to get started.</p></header><section><button class="btn">New project</button></section></div>`             |
| Keyboard key    | `<kbd class="kbd">⌘K</kbd>`                                                                                                                                                  |

Overlays render in place, sized to fit the visual, so you can show them open:

| Overlay     | Markup                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dialog      | `<dialog class="dialog" open><div><header><h2>Rename project</h2><p>…</p></header><section>…</section><footer>buttons</footer></div></dialog>`; `class="alert-dialog"` for a confirmation                                          |
| Toast       | `<div class="toaster"><div class="toast" role="status"><div class="toast-content"><section><h2>Saved</h2><p>…</p></section><footer>…</footer></div></div></div>`                                                                   |
| Open menu   | `<div class="dropdown-menu"><button class="btn" data-variant="outline">Options</button><div data-popover aria-hidden="false"><div role="menu"><div role="menuitem">Edit</div><hr role="separator">…</div></div></div>`             |
| Open select | `<div class="select"><button class="btn" data-variant="outline">Apple</button><div data-popover aria-hidden="false"><div role="listbox"><div role="option" data-value="apple" aria-selected="true">Apple</div>…</div></div></div>` |
| Popover     | `<div class="popover"><button class="btn">Share</button><div data-popover aria-hidden="false">…</div></div>`; opens below, or above with `data-side="top"`                                                                         |
| Tooltip     | `data-tooltip="Copy link"` on any element; shows above it, or below with `data-side="bottom"`                                                                                                                                      |

- **Layout:** Tailwind utilities like `flex gap-2 flex-wrap`, `grid grid-cols-2 gap-4`, `p-4`, `rounded-lg`, `border`, `text-sm`, `font-medium`, `text-muted-foreground`, `bg-muted`.
- **Theme colors:** use the theme's color names (`bg-primary`, `text-primary-foreground`, `bg-muted`, `border-border`, `text-destructive`) rather than fixed colors, so visuals work in light and dark mode.
- **Right-to-left:** use direction-aware spacing (`ms-2`, `me-2`, `ps-4`, `pe-4`, `start-0`, `end-0`), not left/right ones.
- **Width:** the player centers a visual at its own width and never stretches it. When the visual is a surface (a card, dialog, screen, form, table, or chart), give its outer element `w-full` plus a cap, so it fills narrow spaces but stays a sensible size in wide ones: `max-w-xs` in option visuals, `max-w-sm` elsewhere. Leave small pieces (a few buttons, a badge, a single field) at their natural size.
- **Phones:** many learners play on a phone, where the lesson column is about 340px wide (option cards about 160px). Lay visuals out tall rather than wide: stack things instead of putting them side by side, keep tables to 3 columns (or turn rows into a list of small cards), and give nothing a fixed width over 320px. A wide picture shrinks to fit the screen, so its labels get small: lay it out to read at phone width.
- **Labeled parts** (a URL, a sentence, an equation, a word split into its pieces, each part named under it): give each part its own column, the part on top and its label centered under it, and space between columns so neither the parts nor the labels run together: `<div class="flex flex-wrap justify-center gap-x-4 gap-y-3 font-mono text-base"><div class="flex flex-col items-center gap-1.5"><span class="border-b-2 border-primary pb-1">github.com</span><span class="font-sans text-xs text-muted-foreground">registered domain</span></div>…</div>`. Mark every part the same way (the same underline weight and style, colored by its role with `border-primary`, `border-chart-2`, `border-muted-foreground`), keep labels to one or two words, and leave the part the question is about marked like the rest. Never place labels with absolute positions or under a single run of text, where they drift away from their parts and collide.
- **Keep visuals small and focused:** show just enough to make the point. Each visual must be under 20 KB.
- **Not allowed** (the checker rejects them): `<script>`, event handlers like `onclick`, `<iframe>`, `<form>`, `<object>`, `<embed>`, `javascript:` links, and anything loaded from an external URL (images, fonts, stylesheets). A visual is a still picture: components show the state you write (open, checked, selected, invalid) and can't be operated.
- **No images or photos in v1.** Draw what you need with HTML/CSS or inline SVG.
- **Drawings in inline SVG** (pictures, not graphs: boxes and arrows are `diagram` visuals, charts are `chart` visuals): color strokes and text with theme classes (`stroke-primary`, `stroke-muted-foreground`, `fill-muted-foreground`, `stroke-border`) so they work in both themes, and add `direction='ltr'` to the `<svg>` so labels don't flip when the player is right to left (SVG ignores `dir`).
- **Icons** come from the built-in kit, never drawn by hand or loaded from a URL: `<span class="icon-[lucide--git-branch]"></span>`, with a set and an icon name joined by `--`. Sets: `lucide` (general interface icons, the player's own), `octicon` (GitHub's icons, so a GitHub lesson shows what the learner will see: `octicon--repo-16`, `octicon--git-pull-request-16`, `octicon--issue-opened-16`), `simple-icons` (brand marks in one color), `logos` (brand logos in their own colors) and `vscode-icons` (file and language icons in color: `vscode-icons--file-type-python`). One-color icons take the text color, so `text-primary`, `text-muted-foreground`, `text-destructive`, or `text-chart-2` colors them and they work in both themes; colored logos keep their colors, so a dark mark like `logos--github-icon` disappears on a dark screen: for a brand mark prefer `simple-icons`, and keep `logos` and `vscode-icons` for marks whose colors are the point (a language, a file type). An icon is 1em, the size of the text next to it (`<span class="inline-flex items-center gap-1.5"><span class="icon-[octicon--git-branch-16]"></span> main</span>`); for a bigger one, set a size (`size-8`, `text-3xl`). Icons decorate: a label always says what the thing is, the `alt` names what the icons show, and a question never asks the learner to recognise an icon they haven't been shown. Publishing rejects an icon name that doesn't exist and suggests the closest.

## Diagrams in D2

- **Pick D2 when the picture is a graph**: things, and arrows or lines between them. The layout engine places the boxes and routes the arrows, so nothing overlaps or drifts. Pick inline SVG in an `html` visual when the picture is a drawing whose shapes and positions carry the meaning (the parts of a cell, a triangle with its angles, a floor plan).
- **Sequence diagrams** (`shape: sequence_diagram` on the first line) show who sends what to whom, in order: a request through a system, a protocol, a handshake, a conversation. Declare the actors first, in the order they should appear, then the messages, one per line, in the order they happen (`browser -> server: GET /lessons`). `a -> a: thinks` is a message to oneself, `a.note: Waits for the reply` pins a note to an actor, and a container groups a run of messages under a label (`login: Login { a -> b: hello }`; the actors inside it must be declared above it). Keep to 2 to 4 actors (each adds a column, and phones are narrow) and 3 to 8 messages.
- **Grids** (`grid-rows` or `grid-columns` in a container) place things in rows and columns instead of a graph: a timeline, the stages of a process in a row, a few cells to compare, a layout. The cells are ordinary boxes; `grid-gap: 0` joins them into a strip, and a label with a line break (`"1200 BC\nTrade thrives"`) puts a date or name above its text. Keep a row to 3 cells, or use `grid-columns: 1` to stack them, so the grid reads on a phone. Arrows inside a grid draw as straight lines between centers, so use a grid for placement and a flow for what connects.
- **Tables** (`shape: sql_table`) show a record or a database table: each line is `name: type`, and `{constraint: primary_key}`, `foreign_key`, or `unique` marks a key (drawn as PK, FK, UNQ). An arrow from a column to another table's column (`users.team_id -> teams.id`) shows a relation. Keep to 3 to 6 columns per table and 2 or 3 tables.
- **Class diagrams** (`shape: class`) show the classes of a program, UML style: each line is a field (`+name: string`) or a method (`+speak(): string`), with `+` public, `-` private, and `#` protected (quote a protected line, `"#id": int`, since `#` otherwise starts a comment and the line disappears). Mark the relation on the arrow the UML way: `Dog -> Animal: extends {target-arrowhead.shape: triangle; target-arrowhead.style.filled: false}` for inheritance (the hollow triangle at the parent), and `Owner <-> Pet: has {source-arrowhead.shape: diamond; target-arrowhead.shape: arrow}` for "has" (the hollow diamond at the owner; add `source-arrowhead.style.filled: true` when the parts can't exist without it). Write `<->`, not `->`, for the diamond: D2 draws nothing at the start of a one-way arrow. Keep to 2 to 4 classes and 2 to 5 lines each.
- **Keep it small:** 3 to 8 boxes, labels of 1 to 4 words, an arrow label only where the arrow's meaning isn't obvious. A diagram wider than the lesson column is scaled down, and its text with it, most of all on a phone (about 340px): prefer `direction: down` (the default), and use `direction: right` only for a chain of 3 boxes or fewer. Split anything bigger into two katas.
- **Show the point, not everything:** one container for things that belong together, `style.bold: true` or `style.stroke-width: 3` on the one box the kata is about, `style.stroke-dash: 4` for something hypothetical or optional.
- **Right-to-left lessons:** write `direction: left` for a flow that reads across, so it runs the way the text does.
- **Icons on boxes:** `icon: set/name` from the same kit as HTML visuals (`icon: octicon/repo-16`, `icon: lucide/database`, `icon: logos/github-icon`). Use them when the thing has a recognisable picture (a repo, a branch, a database, a browser, a language), on every box of a kind or none, not as decoration on one. The player draws the icon at 32px in the lesson's text color.
- **Where the icon goes:** the label sits at the top of the box and the icon under it, and that default is the one that reads well. `icon.near` can move the icon (`top-left` … `bottom-right`, or `outside-top-center` and the like for outside the box) and `label.near` the label, but a `top-*` icon lands on the label, and a side position (`center-left`, `center-right`) makes D2 widen every box so the whole drawing shrinks to fit. Use them only for one box that needs it, never as the house style.
- **The icon as the box:** `shape: image` with an icon draws only the icon, with the label under it, 56px wide unless you set `width` and `height` (48 to 80 reads well). Good for a few things in a row, like the steps of a flow; use labelled boxes when the name matters more than the picture.
- **Not allowed** (publishing rejects them): colors (the player draws every diagram in the lesson's colors, in light and dark), icons from a URL, `link`, `tooltip`, HTML or images in Markdown labels, and a box or container named with a D2 keyword: `steps`, `layers`, and `scenarios` move their contents to another page, so the lesson would show nothing (call it `stages`, `timeline`, or `phases`); `vars`, `classes`, `label`, `shape`, `style`, `near`, and `direction` are settings, not boxes.

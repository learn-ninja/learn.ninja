# Beginner

For someone who has never done this, or only a little. Assume everyday knowledge and nothing from the field: no terms, no notation, no method. The learner wants one rule they can use today.

## Summary and objective

- Summary: what they'll be able to do, with no jargon: "See why money grows faster when interest earns interest."
- Objective: one rule, applied: "Calculate compound growth year by year."
- Don't list prerequisites; there are none. If the input assumes some, teach the smallest piece needed, or pick a smaller idea.

## Hook

A guess that exposes the everyday intuition the idea corrects, in concrete terms: "A price rises 50%, then falls 50%. Is it back where it started?"

## Concepts

- Name the idea, state the rule in one sentence, then one worked example with real values. Define every term the moment it appears, and bold the key one.
- Explain, don't justify: why the rule holds can wait. Skip history, variants, and exceptions.
- A visual shows the one thing the rule is about and nothing else.

## Questions

- Ask the learner to recognise and understand: which is it, what does this mean, what happens next in a case that looks like the example.
- Natural bricks: `choice`; `match` for the new terms and their meanings; `sort` into two named groups; `compare` of two clearly different examples; `blank` with chips for the key word.
- Wrong options are a newcomer's common slips: tempting, but clearly wrong once the rule is applied (the simple-interest total, the literal translation). Feedback names the slip and restates the rule.
- Hints on most questions, and generous: point at the rule to apply or the first step. Nobody should get stuck.

## Challenge

The rule applied to one new, concrete case the lesson hasn't shown; if there are two concepts, both. Still one step: harder means new, not tricky.

## Takeaways

The rule and when to use it, in plain words. One can name the next idea.

## You've drifted if…

- A term appears before it's explained, or a concept relies on a method it never showed.
- A wrong option could only be picked by someone who didn't read.
- The challenge needs an idea the lesson didn't teach, or only restates a concept sentence instead of applying it to a new case.
